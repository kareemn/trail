TRAIL: Tamper-evident Records for Agent Interactions and Liveness

Abstract

TRAIL makes agent-to-agent and agent-to-tool communication auditable and revocable. An enforcement point outside the agent, the edge, signs each call as a COSE Action Record. That one record authenticates the call, lets the receiver prove what it was sent, and is appended to a tamper-evident trail that witnesses countersign. The edge lets an agent's traffic through only while it holds current state from every authority it answers to and a recent witness countersignature on its trail. TRAIL defines bindings to Media over QUIC Transport (MOQT) and to HTTP. Both produce the same evidence. The MOQT binding also binds each record to exactly one Object, pushes holds to edges as soon as they are published, and delivers trails to authorized parties the operator did not enumerate.¶

1. Introduction

An agent is a model running in a loop with tools, credentials, and a goal. It reads untrusted input, acts with real authority, and works faster than people can watch. Two things are needed outside the agent:¶

  1. An enforcement point that the agent cannot see, reach, or turn off.¶

  2. A record of what the agent did that the operator cannot quietly rewrite, that parties outside the operator can check, and that those parties can act on while the agent is still running.¶

Single-node sandboxes and gateways provide the first. The second is missing as soon as one agent's call reaches an agent on another node.¶

1.1. Problem Statement

Agent calls cross operator boundaries, but the controls and the records for those calls do not. Today:¶

  • A receiver cannot verify, per call, which workload sent it, on whose behalf, under which policy, and whether it was altered in transit by an intermediary such as a relay or proxy.¶

  • Records of agent actions are written by the same software and operator whose conduct they describe, so they can be dropped, reordered, or rewritten without detection.¶

  • Parties with oversight rights cannot observe activity while it happens, pause or stop it, or require approval before an action takes effect. Revoking an agent is hardest when the agent is no longer reachable.¶

  • Limits that apply across many nodes, such as a cap on concurrent agents in a swarm, cannot be enforced because no node sees the total.¶

  • Sharing records with one party exposes them to every party, because there is no way to disclose part of a record to only those entitled to it.¶

1.2. Goals

TRAIL aims to provide the following properties for agent calls that cross node boundaries:¶

Integrity:

Any change to a call, its response, or its record after signing is detectable.¶

Origin:

Every call and record is attributable to an edge and, through it, to a workload identity and a principal.¶

Completeness:

Omitted, reordered, or rewritten records are detectable by parties other than the operator.¶

Policy binding:

Each record names the policy and the delegated authority under which the action was allowed.¶

Causality:

A response, and any action it triggers, can be tied to the call that caused it, across nodes.¶

Liveness:

An edge stops an agent's traffic when it loses contact with the authorities it answers to, or when its trail is no longer being witnessed.¶

Live oversight:

Authorities, including ones the operator did not enumerate, can watch, gate, hold, and stop activity while it runs.¶

Bounded scope:

Limits that span many nodes can be counted and enforced.¶

Scoped disclosure:

Each party sees only the parts of a record it is entitled to.¶

Media evidence:

Media produced or consumed by agents can be bound to the same records.¶

1.3. Design Principles

One artifact:

A single signed object, the Action Record, serves as per-message proof of possession for the receiver, as evidence for auditors, and, when a response cites it, as the counterparty's acknowledgment.¶

The evidence comes from outside the agent:

The edge holds the keys and signs. How far a record can be trusted follows from where the edge sits, which a verifier attests (Section 2.1), not from anything the agent asserts.¶

Two bindings, one evidence format:

Agent calls travel over MOQT, through the agentproto MOQT binding [PACE] and MCP over MOQT [MCP-MOQT], or over HTTP. Records, trails, state, and the rules that act on them are the same in both. MOQT is preferred for the evidence plane (Section 2.4).¶

Three track kinds per party:

Every party (edge, authority, witness, operator) publishes a records track, a state track, and directed tracks. There are no per-function namespaces.¶

Two freshness rules:

An edge must hold a fresh state snapshot from each authority it answers to, and its trail must hold a fresh witness cosignature. These two rules provide kill-switch reachability, monitoring leases, heartbeats, and checkpoint liveness.¶

1.4. Scope

This document specifies:¶

  • The roles and architecture (Section 2), including what the attestation levels mean (Section 2.1) and why MOQT is preferred (Section 2.4).¶

  • The Action Record, its claims, and its signature (Section 3).¶

  • The core rule: no record, no action (Section 4).¶

  • The MOQT binding (Section 5) and the HTTP binding (Section 6).¶

  • How TRAIL attaches to PACE, to MCP over MOQT, and to agent protocols over HTTP (Section 7).¶

  • Trails, checkpoints, and witnesses (Section 8).¶

  • State snapshots, directives, and the freshness rules (Section 9).¶

  • Gates, admission, scoped disclosure, and media evidence (Section 10).¶

  • Identity, delegation, and attestation profiles (Section 11).¶

  • Authorization to publish and read TRAIL tracks (Section 12).¶

This document does not specify:¶

  • Agent application protocols. A2A [A2A], MCP [MCP], and others keep their message formats.¶

  • Dialog management. TRAIL consumes the agentproto dialog context; it does not define it.¶

  • Agent behavior, decision-making, policy languages, inspector models, or the detection of harmful content. TRAIL records actions and enforces rules at the edge; it defines no way to judge whether an action is safe. An authority that judges actions with its own tools acts through TRAIL: a gate before an action (Section 10.1), which is a protocol-level confirmation step, a policy at the edge as it happens, and a hold afterwards (Section 9.1).¶

  • New MOQT messages.¶

1.6. Terminology

This document uses the terms Track, Track Namespace, Full Track Name, Group, Object, Subgroup, Location, Property, Immutable Properties, Original Publisher, Relay, and Fetch from [MoQ-TRANSPORT]; Signed Statement and Transparency Service from [RFC9943]; Workload Identity Token (WIT) from [WIMSE-CREDS]; and Attestation Result and Verifier from [RFC9334].¶

Agent:

Software that uses an AI model in a loop, with tools and credentials, to pursue a goal for a principal.¶

Sandbox:

The isolated environment an agent runs in, such as a container or a microVM. TRAIL does not define sandboxes. It requires that every path out of one pass through an edge.¶

Edge:

Where traffic leaves a cell. Every call into or out of the cell passes through it. It holds the keys, evaluates policy, signs Action Records, appends them to its trail, and applies holds. The agent cannot reach it. When an edge is split across components, they act as one edge.¶

Cell:

Everything behind one edge. Depending on where the edge is (Section 2.1), a cell is one sandbox, a confidential VM, or a whole machine. The cell is the unit at which TRAIL enforces.¶

Party:

Any entity that publishes TRAIL tracks: an edge, an authority, a witness, an operator, or an admission issuer. One party can hold several roles.¶

Operator:

The organization that runs cells and agents, and grants authorities their rights.¶

Principal:

The person or organization an agent acts for.¶

Authority:

A party with rights over some scope that an operator has granted, such as a regulator, a counterparty's inspector, or the operator's own safety team.¶

Witness:

A party that checks that it has received every record in a trail and countersigns the trail's checkpoints. It has no rights over actions (Section 2.2).¶

Action Record:

A signed record by an edge of one action (Section 3). It is a SCITT Signed Statement.¶

Trail:

The append-only sequence of Action Records of one party, organized as a Merkle tree (Section 8).¶

Checkpoint:

An Action Record that signs the size and root of its own trail at the end of an epoch, covering every record before it.¶

Epoch:

The span of a trail that ends with a checkpoint. Over MOQT, an epoch is one Group of the records track.¶

Archive:

A service that stores trails and serves them to authorized readers (Section 8.4).¶

Evidence plane:

The tracks, or HTTP resources, that carry trails, state, and directed records between parties.¶

Acknowledgment:

The responder's Action Record that cites a request's record. It is not a COSE Receipt [RFC9942].¶

Directed record:

A record published by one party for one other party, which can be encrypted to it: gate requests, approvals, admission tokens, disclosures, requests for disclosure, and cosignatures (Section 5.3).¶

State:

A party's current signed snapshot, republished on a schedule (Section 9.1).¶

Directive:

A rule in an authority's state snapshot: a policy reference, a hold, or a requirement (Section 9.1).¶

Grant:

A right an operator gives an authority: which directive types it may issue, for what scope, and which data classes it may read.¶

Action class:

A deployment-defined number that groups actions by impact, such as payments. Selectors and freshness budgets select actions by class.¶

Data class:

A deployment-defined number that labels a redacted claim, such as an amount. Grants list the data classes an authority may read.¶

Hold:

A directive that tells edges to stop actions that match a selector.¶

Gate:

An action that waits for a signed approval of its request.¶

Admission issuer, admission token:

A party that hands out a fixed number of tokens, and the token an agent's records cite (Section 10.2).¶

Attestation level:

software, tee, or isolated: who can read the edge's key, and what can go around the edge (Section 2.1).¶

Swarm:

A set of cells and parties that share one TRAIL deployment and one top-level namespace.¶

2. Architecture

The agent never holds a signing key. For each consequential action the edge:¶

  1. checks the action against cached state from its authorities;¶

  2. signs an Action Record bound to the outgoing call;¶

  3. appends the record to its trail; and¶

  4. sends the call with the record attached.¶

The receiving edge verifies the attached record, delivers the call, and appends a record that cites it. Once the receiver holds the sender's state, verification needs no network round trip; on first contact it needs one subscription or fetch of that state. Authorities and witnesses read trails. Directives flow back in the authorities' state.¶

2.1. Attestation Levels

The agent always runs in a sandbox. What differs is where the edge sits, and therefore what the cell contains, who can read the edge's key, and what can go around the edge. TRAIL names three levels. The level is never asserted by the edge itself: it is derived from an Attestation Result [RFC9334] about the edge's key (Section 11.4).¶

Table 1: Attestation levels
Level Where the edge is What the cell is Who can read the key What goes around the edge
software A process beside the sandbox on the same host, such as a sidecar proxy or egress gateway The sandbox and the edge The host An escape from the sandbox
tee Inside a confidential VM, with its key sealed to that VM The confidential VM Not the host An escape into the VM
isolated Separate hardware that all of the machine's traffic crosses, such as a DPU or SmartNIC The whole machine Nothing on the host Only compromise of that hardware

A key held in a TPM on an ordinary host cannot be exported, but the host can still use it to sign. An edge with such a key is at the software level.¶

The edge is well placed to produce these records. It is on the path, so the trail is complete; it holds keys outside the agent, so a compromised agent cannot forge its own records; it appends each record before the call leaves; and a verifier can attest what holds its key. At the software level a record still shows integrity and binds the operator to what it signed, but a compromised host can sign anything. Authorities can require a higher level for higher-impact actions with a require-level directive.¶

2.2. Authorities and Witnesses

TRAIL separates two jobs:¶

  • A witness checks the completeness and consistency of trails. It needs every record, but only its structure: identifiers, order, and redacted claims. It has no rights over actions. Its cosignatures affect actions only through an authority's require-witness directive (Section 9.3).¶

  • An authority sets rules, issues holds, approves gated actions, and reads the claims its grant covers.¶

Keeping them apart gives least privilege, lets witnesses check authorities as well as operators, and lets several witnesses back one authority. One party MAY hold both roles; a monitor that both checks a trail and can stop the agent it records is an authority and a witness at once.¶

2.3. Dialogs

Agent interactions are grouped into dialogs: the correlated sequence of interactions among participants over the course of a task, named by a dialog identifier and a generation and managed by a controller [PACE]. TRAIL does not manage dialogs. It records the actions taken inside them:¶

  • Every Action Record for an action inside a dialog carries the dialog identifier and the generation the sender last knew (Section 3.2). Records of all participants can then be correlated across cells and operators.¶

  • Dialog control operations that change the dialog or stop work are consequential actions and SHOULD be recorded. Changes to dialog membership are then witnessed.¶

  • A hold is applied by the edge regardless of dialog state. It does not depend on the dialog's controller.¶

  • Where a dialog requires evidence of approval for a high-risk operation, a TRAIL approval record (Section 10.1) is that evidence.¶

  • TRAIL namespaces never contain dialog identifiers. A binding that puts them in its own track names, as PACE does, protects them with its own relay authorization.¶

Section 7.2 maps these rules onto PACE.¶

2.4. Why MOQT

Every TRAIL property is available over HTTP, with the losses listed in Section 6.5. MOQT is preferred for the evidence plane, and for calls where both sides support it, because of properties that MOQT has and HTTP does not:¶

Exact binding:

A MOQT Object has a stable name and address (Full Track Name, Group, Object) and does not change once published [MoQ-TRANSPORT]. An Action Record can therefore bind to exactly one call. An HTTP request has no such name, and a record can only cover a hash of selected components.¶

Evidence that cannot be stripped:

Only the Original Publisher can add Immutable Properties, relays forward them unchanged, and Secure Objects [MoQ-SECOBJ] authenticates them end to end. An HTTP intermediary can drop a header field.¶

One commit point:

The edge writes the record to its trail and publishes the call on the same session, through the same first-hop relay, so "appended before the call leaves" (Section 5.5) is a local ordering rule.¶

Push to parties the publisher did not enumerate:

SUBSCRIBE_TRACKS on a namespace prefix lets authorities, witnesses, and archives receive every trail in a swarm, including trails of cells that appear later, without the publisher knowing who they are. Over HTTP the same result needs discovery through witness trails and repeated fetches (Section 6.2).¶

Pushed state:

A subscription with FILL_PARAMETERS delivers the latest snapshot and every later one, so a hold takes effect when it is published. Over HTTP it takes effect at the edge's next poll.¶

History and live data under one name:

The same records track serves live records by SUBSCRIBE and past ones by FETCH, from relays or archives.¶

One authorization system and one key:

C4M with DPoP [MoQ-C4M] authorizes call tracks and TRAIL tracks with the same WIT-bound key and the same prefix rule.¶

Media:

Agents that see and hear receive audio and video as MOQT Objects in the same dialog, so media seals and key release (Section 10.4) use the same tracks and relays as the rest of the evidence.¶

3. The Action Record

An Action Record is a COSE_Sign1 [RFC9052] structure. It is a SCITT Signed Statement [RFC9943], so it can also be registered with a Transparency Service without conversion. It is the same in both bindings except for the call-ref member.¶

3.1. Protected Header

The protected header MUST contain:¶

  • alg (1): an asymmetric signature algorithm. ES256 is mandatory to implement.¶

  • typ (16) [RFC9596]: application/trail-record+cbor.¶

  • kid (4): the JWK Thumbprint of the edge key, which is the cnf key of the edge's WIT (Section 11).¶

  • CWT Claims (15) [RFC9597] with at least:¶

    • iss (1): the edge's WIMSE identifier.¶

    • sub (2): the identifier of the agent that acted.¶

    • iat (6): the time of signing.¶

3.2. Payload

The payload is a CBOR [RFC8949] map, described in CDDL [RFC8610]:¶

The context-hashes member follows the WIMSE Workload Proof Token [WIMSE-WPT], which binds a proof to the tokens presented with it by hash: wth for the WIT, tth for a Transaction Token, and oth for other context tokens such as an access token. An Action Record is, in effect, a Workload Proof Token for one call that is also kept as evidence.¶

A cite's hash is the record identifier of the cited record, except in a media-group cite, where it is the media root (Section 10.4). A directive cite names the policy bundle record in the authority's trail. The authority snapshots that the edge held are listed in member 14, not in cites.¶

Records and state snapshots MUST use deterministic encoding (Section 4.2.1 of [RFC8949]). The record identifier is the SHA-256 hash of the COSE_Sign1 encoded with an empty unprotected header map. Inclusion proofs and other material MAY be added to the unprotected header, as in SCITT, without changing the identifier.¶

3.3. Signature Binding

trail-ref binds the record to one position in the issuer's trail, and call-ref binds it to one call. A moqt-ref names one immutable Object at one Location; an http-ref covers a hash of the signed components of one request or response (Section 6.1). A relay, proxy, or archive therefore cannot serve the record at another position, and the record cannot be attached to another call. Verifiers MUST check both against where they received the record and the call.¶

3.4. Selective Disclosure

Sensitive claims, such as amounts, principal identifiers, or tool arguments, SHOULD be redacted with SD-CWT [SD-CWT]. The signed record then holds salted digests, which every verifier can check. Each disclosure is labeled with a data class. The edge encrypts the disclosures of a class to each authority whose grant includes that class with COSE-HPKE [COSE-HPKE], and sends them as directed records (Section 5.3). Every authority verifies the same signed record, and each opens only the claims of its classes. Every party that can read the trail, including witnesses, sees only the redacted record.¶

This construction is the CBOR equivalent of the SD-JWT construction [RFC9901] used by SD-Card [SD-CARD], so capability cards and evidence share one disclosure model.¶

4. The Core Rule

Agent-to-agent and agent-to-tool traffic MUST NOT leave a cell unless an Action Record for it has been appended to the edge's trail. An edge MUST drop inbound agent traffic that does not carry a valid Action Record.¶

Table 2: Core rule at the edge
Destination Edge behavior
Agent or tool reached over MOQT Check, sign, append to trail, publish the call Object with TRAIL_RECORD
Agent or tool reached over HTTP As above, with the record carried as in Section 6.1
Model endpoint Record a model-call record; the payload is not forwarded to the evidence plane
Anything else Deny by default

Agent logic does not change. Routine checks run against state already cached at the edge (Section 9) and do not wait on the network. Only gated actions wait for a round trip.¶

During incremental deployment, an operator's policy MAY admit unsigned inbound traffic from named endpoints that do not run TRAIL. The edge then records a recv record with outcome unsigned. A require-record directive from an authority removes that exception for the senders, receivers, and action classes it selects, so removing a record in transit makes the call fail rather than bypass oversight.¶

The receiving edge MUST:¶

  1. Drop the call if its Action Record is missing.¶

  2. Verify the record signature with the key named by kid, obtained from the sender's state (Section 9).¶

  3. Check that call-ref matches the call as received, and that aud, if present, names the receiver.¶

  4. Check the sender against the receiver's requirements: attestation level, holds, admission, the status of the tokens named in context-hashes, and upstream freshness from the cosigned checkpoint in the sender's state (Section 9.3). The receiver cannot observe the sender's downstream freshness directly. The sender declares the authority snapshots it held in member 14; a receiver that holds those authorities' state MUST check that each named snapshot exists and is within the applicable budget.¶

  5. Reject a record identifier it has already accepted, unless the action is idempotent.¶

  6. Deliver the call, and append a recv record that cites the sender's record.¶

A response carries the responder's record, which cites the request's record. The requester keeps it as the responder's acknowledgment. Each side can then prove what the other said, which is the basis for assigning liability between them.¶

5. MOQT Binding

5.1. Requirements on MOQT

Implementations MUST support [MoQ-TRANSPORT] draft-22 or later, including PUBLISH_NAMESPACE, SUBSCRIBE_TRACKS, PUBLISH, SUBSCRIBE with FILL_PARAMETERS, FETCH, TRACK_STATUS, Immutable Properties, and the AUTHORIZATION TOKEN parameter with C4M tokens [MoQ-C4M].¶

Records, state, and directed tracks MUST use Subgroup Delivery Mode, MUST NOT use Datagram Delivery Mode, and MUST NOT set a delivery timeout.¶

An edge MUST NOT carry anything that a TRAIL check depends on in a mutable Property.¶

5.2. Namespaces

Every TRAIL track lives under the publishing party's prefix:¶

S is the swarm pseudonym. P and Q are party pseudonyms. All three MUST be 16-byte values that do not encode identities, locations, or tasks. The mapping from a pseudonym to a WIMSE identifier is in that party's state snapshot, which only authorized parties can read (Section 12).¶

Call Objects stay on their dialog tracks, for example ("pace", "dialogs", {dialog-id}, {agent-id}, "a2a", "task") -- {task-id} [PACE]. Media stays on its dialog media tracks.¶

An authority sees the whole swarm with one SUBSCRIBE_TRACKS on ("trail", S), or one party with SUBSCRIBE_TRACKS on ("trail", S, P). Because a cell whose records track is not published under ("trail", S) gets no cosignatures and fails upstream freshness, an operator cannot start a cell that such a subscription will not see.¶

5.3. The Three Track Kinds

records:

The party's trail. Each Object is one Action Record. A Group is one epoch and ends with a checkpoint (Section 8.2). Object IDs within a Group start at 0 and are consecutive. The publisher MUST NOT use the Prior Object ID Gap Property on a records track, so any gap is evidence of loss or withholding.¶

state:

The party's current snapshot. Each Group holds exactly one Object, a complete signed snapshot (Section 9.1). A receiver never merges deltas. The track SHOULD use Descending group order. Subscribers join with FILL_PARAMETERS selecting the latest Group, so they get current state and live updates in one step.¶

to:

Directed records addressed to one other party: gate requests and approvals, admission tokens, disclosures, and requests for disclosure. Payloads that are not public MUST be encrypted to the recipient with COSE-HPKE. Every directed record that matters as evidence is also appended, in redacted form, to the sender's trail.¶

5.4. The TRAIL_RECORD Property

TRAIL defines one Object Property, TRAIL_RECORD (odd type, byte value), whose value is the Action Record for that Object.¶

  • The edge MUST place it inside Immutable Properties, or in the Encrypted Properties List when the call track is protected with Secure Objects [MoQ-SECOBJ], so that relays cannot see it.¶

  • Relays forward it unchanged, as they do any unknown Property. A relay cannot add it, because MOQT does not let relays add Immutable Properties. This is why the edge must be the Original Publisher.¶

  • Because the record is carried with the Object, the receiver needs no FETCH and no second subscription to verify a call.¶

5.5. Commit Point

MOQT does not acknowledge individual Objects. TRAIL therefore defines the commit point as follows:¶

  • The edge MUST write the record to its records track before it releases the call Object. Deployments SHOULD make the edge its own first-hop relay, so the record is cached before the call leaves.¶

  • The receiver verifies the record inline (it is in the Object).¶

  • The checkpoint for the epoch MUST include the record. A receiver that holds a record whose leaf is covered by a cosigned checkpoint that does not include it has proof of equivocation.¶

  • For actions above a policy threshold, the edge MAY wait until a witness has cosigned a checkpoint that includes the record before it releases the call (synchronous mode).¶

6. HTTP Binding

Most agent traffic today is HTTP [RFC9110]: MCP [MCP] commonly uses its Streamable HTTP transport, and A2A [A2A] uses JSON-RPC over HTTP. The HTTP binding lets that traffic carry TRAIL without changing the agents.¶

6.1. Calls

The edge signs the request with WIMSE HTTP Message Signatures [WIMSE-HTTPSIG] [RFC9421] on the agent's behalf and adds a Trail-Record header field: an Item Structured Field [RFC9651] whose value is a Byte Sequence holding the Action Record.¶

The keyid is the JWK Thumbprint of the edge key, the same value as the record's kid.¶

  • The signature MUST cover @method, @target-uri, content-digest [RFC9530] when the message has content, and trail-record.¶

  • components-hash in the record's http-ref is the SHA-256 hash of the signature base for the covered components other than trail-record, without the @signature-params line. created equals the signature's created parameter.¶

  • The edge MUST request a signed response. The responder's edge returns its own record, citing the request's record, in a Trail-Record response field covered by its response signature.¶

  • When the response is a stream, such as server-sent events [SSE] on an MCP Streamable HTTP response, the responder's record is carried in the event that completes the exchange and lists the hashes of the earlier events.¶

6.2. Trails as Static Tiles

A party publishes its trail as a tiled transparency log [TLOG-TILES]. Tiles use the same [RFC9162] Merkle tree as TRAIL trails; entry bundles hold the records in leaf order, and the last record of each epoch is the checkpoint (Section 8.2). The trail's checkpoint resource is a signed tree head in the [TLOG-CHECKPOINT] format over the same tree, so existing tlog tooling can read TRAIL trails directly.¶

Tiles are immutable once full and can be served from caches. Readers authenticate as described in Section 12.2 to a server that the operator whose trail it is does not run, so the operator does not learn who reads its trail.¶

A reader finds trails through witnesses. A cell obtains cosignatures by submitting each checkpoint to its witnesses, for example with [TLOG-WITNESS], and each witness records the countersignature, with the cell's trail identifier and base URI, in its own trail. An authority that reads a witness's trail therefore learns every cell that witness cosigns, including cells started later.¶

6.3. State and Directed Records

A party serves its current state snapshot at a state resource listed in its state, with an ETag. Readers poll it with conditional requests or keep an SSE stream open on which each new snapshot is an event. Directed records are delivered by POST to the recipient's inbox resource.¶

The freshness rules are unchanged, because the edge checks the age of what it holds, not how it arrived. An authority's freshness budget for a class MUST exceed the edge's poll interval plus the expected delivery latency, or the edge will pause between polls. A hold therefore takes effect within one poll interval, instead of on publication.¶

6.4. Commit Point

The trail append and the call can be on two systems. The edge MUST receive a successful acknowledgment of the append before it sends the call.¶

Calls and the evidence plane can use different bindings. In a hybrid deployment, agent calls stay on HTTP and trails, state, and directed records travel over MOQT, so only edges, authorities, and witnesses need MOQT. This is the RECOMMENDED first deployment for agents that use HTTP today. A verifier checks a record the same way whichever binding carried it.¶

7. Carrying TRAIL in Agent Protocols

TRAIL does not change agent protocols. It attaches to the messages that their bindings already carry. This section states how for the agentproto MOQT binding [PACE], for MCP over MOQT [MCP-MOQT], and for A2A and MCP over HTTP.¶

7.1. The General Rule

In every mapping, the edge acts for the agent and is the sender of the agent's messages. For each message that carries a request, a response, a result, or a control operation, the edge attaches an Action Record: TRAIL_RECORD over MOQT (Section 5.4), or Trail-Record over HTTP (Section 6.1). Messages that carry only progress, keep-alive, or streaming fragments MAY omit it, provided the message that completes the exchange carries a record whose call-ref covers it and whose body lists the hashes of the fragments it completes.¶

The protocol's own identifiers (PACE message_id, A2A task identifiers, MCP JSON-RPC id, and MCP over MOQT client ID) SHOULD be recorded in the record body, so that the evidence can be matched to the protocol's own logs. They are not used for any TRAIL check.¶

7.2. PACE

7.2.1. Layering

TRAIL is not a layer of the PACE stack. It runs at the edge, beside PACE. It attaches an Action Record to each PACE Object the edge publishes, and it keeps its own tracks under ("trail", S, P) on the same MOQT relays.¶

PACE correlates a dialog and manages its lifecycle. TRAIL proves what each participant did in it and enforces rules at the edge. PACE defines no audit data structures; TRAIL supplies them, keyed by the PACE dialog identifier.¶

Table 5: Division of work between PACE and TRAIL

7.2.2. Mapping

Dialog tracks:

Call Objects stay on the participant's dialog tracks, for example ("pace", "dialogs", {dialog-id}, {agent-id}, "a2a", "task") -- {task-id}. TRAIL tracks are separate, under ("trail", S, P). The PACE dialog-id and agent-id never appear in TRAIL namespaces.¶

Dialog context:

Every record for an Object on a dialog track carries dialog-ref with the PACE dialog-id and the generation the sender last knew. These are the values of the dialog reference that the application message carries under org.ietf.agentproto/dialog-context. A receiving edge MUST refuse a call whose dialog reference and dialog-ref differ.¶

Control operations:

Each control message on a participant's ctrl track that changes the dialog or stops work (OPEN, ACCEPT, REJECT, UPDATE, JOIN_REQUEST, LEAVE, REVOKE, TERMINATE, CANCEL, and CANCEL_ACK) SHOULD carry a lifecycle record. STATE_REQUEST, STATE, and ERROR MAY omit it. PACE requires a participant to authenticate the sender of each operation before applying it, and relays terminate TLS on each hop. TRAIL_RECORD authenticates each control Object end to end, and the trail makes changes to dialog membership witnessed.¶

Payload:

PACE carries each message as the Object payload, with no PACE header. payload-hash covers the whole Object payload.¶

Credentials:

Identity assertions, authorization proofs, and SD-Card presentations travel in the PACE credentials member, as PACE specifies. The record carries their hashes in context-hashes. A key-bound presentation is bound to a nonce from the peer, so it travels on ACCEPT, UPDATE, or JOIN_REQUEST, never on the card track.¶

Confirmation and evidence:

Where PACE lets a peer require evidence of approval for a high-risk operation, a TRAIL approval record (Section 10.1) is the evidence. The receiving edge finds it as a cite of type approval in the sender's record.¶

Holds, REVOKE, and CANCEL:

When a hold stops a participant, the controller SHOULD follow with REVOKE so that the dialog context matches, and the edge MUST keep applying the hold until it is lifted. An edge that holds work already in progress SHOULD send CANCEL for it, so that peers stop work they started for the held agent. A CANCEL_ACK with outcome irreversible is recorded like any other control message, so the trail shows which actions took effect before the stop.¶

Delegated dialogs:

A dialog opened to carry out delegated work lists its parents in parent_dialog_ids. The lifecycle record of OPEN carries the full dialog context, so the trails show which dialogs descend from which.¶

Discovery:

The PACE card track and the TRAIL state track are separate. A cell's state MAY include the issuer-signed SD-Card that the agent also publishes on its card track, so that a receiver can check capability claims and key binding from the same subscription it uses for the edge's credentials.¶

Revocation at the relay:

PACE withdraws a revoked participant's relay authorization, for example by refusing C4M revalidation. A hold at the edge stops the same participant independently of the relay, so either one is enough to stop it.¶

7.3. MCP over MOQT

Request and response tracks:

MCP over MOQT maps each JSON-RPC exchange onto a request track published by the client and a response track published by the server, in the client's scope (mcp, {server-id}, clients, {client-id}, ...). Each request is a new Group, and the response uses the same Group ID. The client's edge attaches a record of kind tool-call (or send for other requests) to the request Object. The server's edge attaches a record that cites the request's record to the Object that carries the final result or error.¶

Track ownership:

In a client scope the client and the server publish under the same namespaces, and only the track name tells a request track from a response track. A receiving edge MUST check that the record's iss is the edge it expects for that track: the client's edge for a request track, the server's edge for a response track. A peer that publishes a track belonging to the other is then refused.¶

Multi-object responses:

A response Group can hold progress notifications, in one or more Subgroups, followed by the final result or error, which has the highest Object ID. The Object that carries the final result MUST carry a record, and the general rule above applies to the notifications, taken in Object ID order. The record travels with its Object, so a client that recovers a response by FETCH after a reconnection verifies it the same way.¶

Multi round-trip requests:

MCP servers do not send requests to clients. A server that needs an elicitation, a sampling result, or the client's roots ends the response with an input-required result, which carries a record like any other result. The client's retry is a new request in a new Group, and its record cites the input-required result's record. Sampling done by the client to answer it is recorded as a model-call record by the client's edge, and the retry cites that record as input. An elicitation that a person answers can be gated (Section 10.1).¶

Cancellation:

A client cancels a request by publishing notifications/cancelled in the request's Group. That Object is a control message and carries a record, as does a PACE CANCEL that names the request.¶

Origin of tool results:

Secure Objects keys are shared by a track's publisher and subscribers, so they do not show which endpoint produced an Object. TRAIL_RECORD is a per-publisher signature, and placing it in the Encrypted Properties List keeps it hidden from relays.¶

Standalone and PACE modes:

In standalone mode there is no dialog. Records omit dialog-ref and record the server ID, the client ID, and the JSON-RPC id in the body; MCP keeps no session, so there is no session identifier to record. In PACE mode the same tracks move under ("pace", "dialogs", {dialog-id}, {agent-id}, "mcp", ...), each MCP request carries the PACE dialog reference in _meta, and records carry dialog-ref as in Section 7.2.¶

Authorization:

MCP over MOQT presents an OAuth access token, or a C4M token minted from the OAuth grant and bound with the DPoP binding of C4M, to the relay. Credentials that the MCP server itself verifies travel in each request's _meta. TRAIL uses the same C4M tokens (Section 12). The access token, or a Transaction Token derived from it, is recorded by hash in oth or tth.¶

Catalog, resources, prompts, and skills:

The catalog, resource, prompt, and skill tracks carry content that the server publishes, not actions, and relays serve them from cache. Tool definitions and client-executed skill instructions steer the model, so an edge SHOULD record a state-read record when an agent uses such content, and the later action cites it. Context provenance then follows data from a server into a decision. A server-executed skill (skills/invoke) and a task are requests like tools/call, and are recorded the same way.¶

7.4. A2A and MCP over HTTP

A2A requests and MCP Streamable HTTP requests carry a Trail-Record field as in Section 6.1. The responder's record is carried in the response, or, for a streamed response, in the event that completes the exchange. When the message carries a PACE dialog reference, in MCP _meta or in A2A message metadata, the record carries the same values in dialog-ref. Records are appended to trails on whichever evidence plane the deployment uses (Section 6.6).¶

8. Trails, Checkpoints, and Witnesses

8.1. The Trail

Each party's trail is a Merkle tree as defined in [RFC9162]. The leaves are record identifiers, in trail order. The leaf index is the leaf value in the record's trail-ref. One trail per cell is the default. An edge MAY run more than one trail, each with its own trail-id and records track, for example one per tenant.¶

An edge that loses its trail state MUST start a new trail with a new trail-id, MUST record a restart record in the new trail that names the old trail-id and last known checkpoint, and MUST NOT reuse an old trail-id. A party changes its pseudonym the same way: it starts a new trail under the new pseudonym, with a restart record that names the old trail-id, so that witnesses keep the history connected.¶

8.2. Checkpoints

The last record of every epoch is a checkpoint: an Action Record of kind checkpoint, signed by the trail's own edge, whose body is a tree head:¶

tree-size and root-hash cover every record before the checkpoint. The checkpoint itself is the leaf at index tree-size, and the next checkpoint covers it.¶

A checkpoint is a record made by the trail producer about its own trail. It is not a COSE Receipt: TRAIL trails are not Transparency Services, and a party proves inclusion of a record with an [RFC9162] inclusion proof against a cosigned checkpoint. A witness that also operates a SCITT Transparency Service MAY additionally register records and issue COSE Receipts [RFC9942] for them; TRAIL does not depend on that.¶

The epoch length is set by policy. A witness MAY ask for an early checkpoint with REQUEST_UPDATE and NEW_GROUP_REQUEST, when the track sets DYNAMIC_GROUPS.¶

8.3. Witnesses

A witness reads a party's trail. For each checkpoint it:¶

  • checks that it holds every record up to the checkpoint's tree size, and fetches any it lacks;¶

  • checks that the new tree is consistent with the last tree head it cosigned for that trail;¶

  • countersigns the checkpoint with a COSE countersignature [RFC9338] whose protected header carries CWT Claims with iat, the time of cosigning; and¶

  • appends a record of kind cosignature to its own trail, whose body is the countersignature with the trail-id and tree size it covers, and sends the countersignature to the party as a directed record.¶

A witness MUST NOT countersign two different roots for the same trail and tree size. Two such countersignatures are proof that the witness misbehaved, and two conflicting checkpoints with countersignatures from different witnesses are proof that the trail's operator equivocated.¶

A cosignature says more than "the root is consistent". It says that the witness has received every record in the tree. TRAIL uses this in place of a separate monitoring lease.¶

The party copies its latest cosigned checkpoint into its state snapshot, so that any peer can check it with one subscription or fetch.¶

8.4. Archives

MOQT relays are not required to cache. Each deployment MUST name at least one archive for every witness. An archive reads trails and serves them by FETCH, or as tiles over HTTP. Witnesses and authorities that miss records fetch them from an archive. A witness MAY be its own archive.¶

8.5. What Makes Records Tamper-Evident

Five properties together make changes to records detectable:¶

  1. Each Action Record is signed by the edge key, so changing any byte invalidates the signature.¶

  2. Each record is a leaf of its trail's Merkle tree, so editing, removing, inserting, or reordering records changes the root.¶

  3. Each checkpoint signs the size and root of the trail, fixing its history up to that point.¶

  4. Witnesses cosign checkpoints and never cosign two roots for the same size, so a party cannot show different histories to different readers without producing proof of equivocation.¶

  5. A counterparty's acknowledgment cites the hash of the record it received and is kept in the counterparty's own trail, so a rewritten record no longer matches what the counterparty holds.¶

Tamper evidence is not tamper proofing. An edge that is compromised at signing time produces validly signed false records. The attestation level bounds how likely that is, and acknowledgments and witnesses bound how far a false record can go unnoticed.¶

9. State, Directives, and Freshness

9.1. State Snapshots

A party's state carries its current self-description, its directives (authorities), and its grants (operators). Each snapshot is a COSE_Sign1 signed by the party's key. Its protected header is the same as an Action Record's, with typ application/trail-state+cbor. Its payload is:¶

A selector matches an action when every member present in it matches. A member matches when any of its values matches. An absent member matches everything.¶

Policy bundles, which can be large, are referenced from directives by hash. The bundle itself is a record in the authority's trail, and edges fetch it once. The policy claim of every Action Record is the SHA-256 hash of the deterministically encoded array of [authority, directive id] pairs for the directives the edge applied, sorted by their encoding, so "it was allowed" can be checked later.¶

An authority's directives apply only within the grant its operator gives it. An edge MUST ignore any directive that falls outside the issuing authority's grant in the operator's current state snapshot. One authority therefore cannot stop a whole swarm unless the operator agreed to that, and the operator's grants are visible to every party that can read its state.¶

Holds that apply to a single credential, such as one agent's WIT or one admission token, SHOULD be expressed as a suspended entry in the issuer's Token Status List [OAUTH-STATUS-LIST], carried in the issuer's state. Revocation is an invalid entry. This uses the same mechanism that OAuth and WIMSE deployments use for credential status.¶

An edge MUST record a hold-applied record the first time it stops an action because of a hold.¶

9.2. How Directives Act

Directives differ in when they take effect:¶

Table 6: How directives act
Directive Takes effect Effect

hold As soon as the edge holds it Matching actions stop
require-level, require-admission, require-record As soon as the edge holds it Calls that do not meet the requirement are refused
require-gate On each matching action The action waits for an approval (Section 10.1)

9.3. The Two Freshness Rules

Downstream freshness (authority to cell):

An edge MUST hold, for every authority that has a grant in its operator's state, a snapshot that is within that authority's freshness budget for the action class (Section 9.4). If it does not, it MUST NOT perform actions of that class covered by the grant, except as allowed by degraded mode (Section 9.5).¶

Upstream freshness (cell to witness):

For every require-witness directive that applies, a cell's latest checkpoint MUST carry countersignatures from at least k of the named witnesses, each with an iat no older than the directive's max-age. If it does not, the edge MUST NOT perform covered actions except as allowed by degraded mode, and receivers MUST reject calls from that cell that the directive covers.¶

An authority keeps its snapshot fresh by republishing it, even when nothing changes. This is the heartbeat, and it proves that the authority can still reach the edge. A witness keeps a cell fresh by cosigning its checkpoints. This proves that the witness is still receiving the cell's trail. An authority that cannot deliver a hold can stop republishing, and the edge stops on its own once the snapshot ages out; revocation therefore works when the agent is unreachable.¶

These two rules replace heartbeats, monitoring leases, checkpoint deadlines, and maximum staleness bounds. For example, the rule "any agent calling service Z must be monitored by regulator R" is a require-witness directive from R, with Z as the counterparty in its selector. If R stops watching, its cosignatures stop, the callers' checkpoints age out, and Z's edge rejects their calls.¶

9.4. Freshness Budgets per Action Class

Failing closed on every action turns an authority outage or a relay partition into an outage of the business. Freshness is therefore set per action class. A freshness-budget directive sets how old the authority's own snapshot may get, and what happens when it is too old. A require-witness directive, which can also select action classes, sets which witnesses must have cosigned, how many of them, and how recently. Each limit is set in exactly one place:¶

When several budgets or witness requirements apply to one action, the edge MUST apply the strictest. When no budget applies, the snapshot's exp is the limit and the behavior is pause. When a require-witness limit is exceeded, the edge uses the behavior and grace period of the freshness-budget that applies to the same action class, or pause if there is none.¶

9.5. Degraded Mode

An action class whose budget says degrade MAY continue for a bounded grace period after the budget is exceeded:¶

  • The grace period, and the classes allowed to degrade, MUST come from the last snapshot the edge received while it was fresh, so an attacker who blocks state cannot widen what continues.¶

  • An operator grant caps the grace period an authority may allow.¶

  • Gated actions and actions covered by a hold never degrade.¶

  • Every degraded action is recorded with outcome degraded, and the next checkpoint lists them.¶

  • When the grace period ends, the class pauses.¶

10. Gates, Admission, Disclosure, and Media

10.1. Gates

For an action matched by a require-gate directive, the edge holds the action and:¶

  • appends a gate-request record to its trail, whose body is the hash of the pending call and any redacted context the approver needs; and¶

  • sends the same record to each approver named in the directive, as a directed record, with disclosures for that approver's classes.¶

The approver publishes an approval record as a directed record for the cell, and appends it to its own trail. The approval cites the gate-request record with a cite of type peer-record and states the decision. The edge continues only after a valid approval from every named approver, and its send record cites each approval.¶

A require-gate selector can cover every action an agent takes. Every such action then waits for a round trip to the approver, and stops if the approver cannot be reached, because gated actions never degrade.¶

The approver can be a person. In that case the approval is signed with a key bound to that person, for example through an OAuth client or a platform authenticator, and the approval record names that person in a redacted claim.¶

10.2. Admission and Counting

Limits that span many cells, such as "no more than 1,000 live agents in swarm S", use admission tokens:¶

  • The admission issuer is a party. It issues each agent an admission token: a CWT [RFC8392] with cnf set to the agent's WIT key, a unique cti, an expiry, and a status claim that points into the issuer's Token Status List [OAUTH-STATUS-LIST]. The token is sent as a directed record for the cell.¶

  • The issuer never has more than the cap of valid tokens at once.¶

  • A require-admission directive tells receivers to accept calls only from agents whose records carry the hash of a valid admission token in context-hashes.¶

  • When an agent retires, its edge appends an admission-release record. The issuer then marks the token invalid and can issue a new one.¶

  • Any witness can count live tokens from the issuer's trail, and the same token cited by two agents shows up in the trails.¶

Runtime limits use the token's expiry, and the C4M moqt-reval claim (Section 12) for relay access. The cap binds only among parties that check tokens; a server that does not run TRAIL does not refuse calls without one.¶

10.3. Disclosure on Request

An authority that needs a claim it was not sent proactively sends a disclosure request as a directed record for the cell. If the authority's grant covers the claim's data class, the edge answers with disclosures encrypted to the authority, as a directed record, and appends a disclosure record to its trail. If it does not, the edge MUST refuse and MUST record the refusal. Every look at protected data, and every refused attempt, is therefore itself a witnessed record.¶

10.4. Media Evidence

Agents that see and hear act on media that is already carried on MOQT tracks. For each media Group the edge publishes, or receives and forwards to the agent, it computes a Merkle root over the (Object ID, payload hash) pairs of the Group, and appends a media-seal record that names the track, the Group, the Object count, and the root.¶

An Action Record that depended on media cites it with a media-group cite that names the track, the Group, and the Object count, with the root as its hash. Changing one frame breaks the link. Inclusion proofs let a verifier check single frames.¶

Live media MAY use Datagram Delivery Mode and delivery timeouts. Evidence checks MUST use complete Groups fetched from an archive.¶

For approval before playback, each media Group uses its own Secure Objects key. The publisher sends the key, encrypted with COSE-HPKE, to the approver as a directed record. When the approver approves, it sends the key to the entitled viewers as directed records. Nothing plays before approval, and each release is a record in the approver's trail.¶

11.1. Identity: WIMSE

WIMSE workload credentials [WIMSE-CREDS] are mandatory to implement.¶

  • Each edge, authority, witness, operator, and admission issuer has a WIMSE identifier and a WIT. The kid of every TRAIL signature is the thumbprint of the signer's WIT cnf key.¶

  • Each agent has its own WIMSE identifier and WIT. The agent's WIT key is held by the edge, never by the agent, consistent with [WIMSE-AIMS]. The record's sub is the agent's identifier and wth is the hash of the agent's WIT.¶

  • WITs are published in the party's state snapshot. A receiver reads the sender's state once and then verifies every call from that sender without further lookups.¶

The WIMSE architecture [WIMSE-ARCH] requires an intermediary that changes identity context to make the change explicit and auditable. The edge acts for the agent, and the Action Record makes that change explicit.¶

Other identity schemes, such as X.509 certificates or SPIFFE SVIDs, can be used through a profile that provides the same four things: a key bound to an operator and an agent, a link to the principal, an attestation result for the key, and a status mechanism.¶

11.2. Principal and Delegation: OAuth

TRAIL does not define a delegation format. The principal and the chain of delegation are carried by OAuth Transaction Tokens [TXN-TOKENS], whose sub, scope, tctx, and rctx claims describe who the call is for, what it may do, and the context of the originating request, or by an access token obtained through Token Exchange [RFC8693] with its act chain. Fine-grained permissions can use Rich Authorization Requests [RFC9396].¶

Every record for an action taken on behalf of a principal MUST bind both the principal and the delegation chain: member 12 identifies the principal, usually redacted, and tth or oth carries the hash of the token that conveys the delegation. The token itself travels in the application message, for example in a PACE dialog's credentials, in MCP _meta, or in an HTTP header. The Transaction Token's txn value SHOULD be recorded, so that evidence and the operator's own transaction logs can be correlated.¶

11.3. Capability: SD-Card

An agent's capability card, for example an SD-Card [SD-CARD], says what the agent claims it can do. TRAIL uses it as follows:¶

  • The card's cnf key SHOULD be the agent's WIT key. One key then covers discovery, workload authentication, relay authorization, and evidence.¶

  • The issuer-signed card, with only its public disclosures and no key binding JWT, MAY be included in the cell's state snapshot.¶

  • Context-specific disclosures and the key binding JWT are per-verifier. They MUST travel inside the dialog (for example in PACE OPEN or ACCEPT), not on a cached track.¶

  • An Action Record records the sd_hash of the presentation the counterparty relied on in sdh.¶

11.4. Attestation: RATS

The attestation level (Table 1) of an edge key MUST come from an Attestation Result [RFC9334] produced by a verifier the receiver trusts, bound to the edge's WIT key, for example as in [WIMSE-ATTEST]. An EAT Attestation Result [RATS-EAR] [RFC9711] wrapped in a Conceptual Message Wrapper [RFC9999] is RECOMMENDED. Trustworthiness claims [RATS-AR4SI] map to levels by deployment policy. The Attestation Result is included in the edge's state snapshot and refreshed before it expires. An edge MUST NOT state its own level.¶

12. Authorization

12.1. Publishing

Every party authenticates with a token bound with DPoP [RFC9449] to its WIT key: over MOQT, a C4M token [MoQ-C4M] whose cnf.jkt is the thumbprint of the WIT key; over HTTP, an OAuth access token. One rule covers publishing: a party may publish only its own trail, its own state, and directed records it sends. Over MOQT, its token grants PUBLISH_NAMESPACE and PUBLISH only under its own prefix ("trail", S, P).¶

12.2. Reading Trails and State

Trails, state, and directed records are not public. Only authorized parties read them, and directed records only by their recipient. Each swarm has an authorization server that issues the tokens for reading its trails and state. How a swarm names it is an open issue (Appendix D). That authorization server SHOULD NOT be controlled by any single operator whose cells it covers, so that the operator cannot choose who oversees it. Every authority that holds a grant from an operator in the swarm, and every witness named in a require-witness directive, MUST be able to obtain read access to the trails and state it needs.¶

Table 8: Read access per role
Role Reads
Cell edge State of its authorities, operator, and peers; directed records addressed to it
Authority, witness Trails and state under ("trail", S), or of the parties in its grant; directed records addressed to it
Archive The trails and state it stores

In C4M terms, a cell edge with pseudonym P gets a scope with actions PUBLISH_NAMESPACE and PUBLISH and namespace match ["trail", S, P], and a second scope with action SUBSCRIBE and track match on its own pseudonym under the to namespaces of its authorities. MOQT does not copy authorization tokens from SUBSCRIBE_TRACKS into the PUBLISH messages it produces, so a relay MUST check each forwarded PUBLISH against the scope of the token presented with the SUBSCRIBE_TRACKS.¶

12.3. Relays

Relays are not trusted for TRAIL's guarantees, but they can help enforce them:¶

  • C4M tokens for agents SHOULD carry moqt-reval and a status claim that refers to the issuer's Token Status List. A relay that revalidates then stops forwarding for a suspended agent, which adds a network-level kill switch to the edge's own.¶

  • An edge relay can check that call Objects carry TRAIL_RECORD. It cannot verify the record. A C4M claim that requires a given Property on every published Object would let relays enforce presence (Appendix D).¶

C4M and DPoP authenticate sessions and actions hop by hop. The Action Record authenticates the content of each action end to end. They are complementary and use the same key.¶

14.2. Parties and Threats

Agent:

Can ask to act. Cannot sign, reach the network except through the edge (subject to Section 14.6), or skip policy.¶

Operator:

Runs cells and chooses relays. Cannot edit records after the fact or show different histories to different witnesses without producing proof of equivocation. Cannot quietly stop publishing, because checkpoints stop being cosigned and the freshness rules pause the cell. Can choose which authorities to grant; that choice is itself in the operator's signed state.¶

Relay or proxy:

Can route, cache, delay, or drop messages, and can change mutable Properties. Cannot forge records or move them, and dropped records show up as gaps. An HTTP proxy can drop the Trail-Record field; under require-record the receiver then refuses the call.¶

Authority:

Can read its granted classes and issue directives within its grant. Cannot read other classes, because disclosures are encrypted to each recipient. Cannot act outside its grant.¶

Hardware root:

Vouches for the keys of tee and isolated edges. It is the one root that every party must trust. Deployments SHOULD accept roots from more than one vendor.¶

14.3. Shared Keys

Secure Objects keys are shared by a track's publisher and subscribers, so any key holder can produce Objects that decrypt. TRAIL never relies on them for origin. Disclosures use public-key encryption to each recipient, so authorities cannot forge disclosures for each other.¶

14.4. Replay

A replayed call carries a record identifier the receiver has already accepted. Receivers MUST keep the identifiers they accept for at least the maximum record age they allow, and reject repeats for actions that are not idempotent. Over HTTP, receivers MUST also reject a created value outside their allowed clock skew. Even when a replay is not caught, the call-ref binding means it can only reproduce the same call. As PACE requires for its control messages, an edge MUST NOT send a call that is not idempotent in QUIC 0-RTT data.¶

14.5. Denial of Service

The freshness rules let an attacker who can block state or cosignatures, or an outage of an authority, a witness, or a relay, pause agents. The edge cannot tell an outage from an attack. TRAIL limits the damage instead: freshness budgets are set per action class (Section 9.4), so an outage first stops high-impact actions; degraded mode (Section 9.5) lets classes the last fresh snapshot allowed continue for a bounded grace period, recorded for later review; and an operator grant bounds how much any one authority can stop. Deployments SHOULD provide redundant relay paths for state and directed records, SHOULD use more than one witness with a k-of-n rule, and SHOULD test each action class under the loss of each authority and witness. Prefix subscriptions near the root of the namespace can cause large numbers of PUBLISH messages, and relays SHOULD limit them through authorization.¶

14.6. Bypass

Everything in TRAIL depends on the edge seeing every call. At the software level, an agent that escapes its sandbox, or a compromised host, can go around the edge. At the tee level, the key is protected, but an agent that escapes into the confidential VM can still go around an edge that runs inside it. Only at the isolated level does hardware in the network path see every packet. Receivers enforce the core rule inbound, so a call that bypassed the edge is refused by every TRAIL peer; bypass reaches only endpoints that do not run TRAIL. Authorities can require the isolated level for high-impact action classes with require-level.¶

14.7. Limits

TRAIL defines no way to judge whether an action is safe. An authority judges with its own tools and acts through TRAIL: a gate before the action, a policy at the edge as it happens, or a hold afterwards. It sees content only through disclosures its grant covers. A signed, approved message can still carry an injection. Covert channels through timing, patterns in ordinary output, or public content are not blocked and can only be found by audit. Interfaces through which a hardware enforcement point exposes its signing key are not standardized.¶

15. Privacy Considerations

A system that can prove what every agent did can also show what every person using those agents did. TRAIL follows [RFC6973] and limits this:¶

  • TRAIL namespaces and resource paths contain only pseudonyms, which a party rotates by starting a new trail (Section 8). WIMSE identifiers, dialog identifiers, and principals appear in TRAIL only inside signed payloads, and sensitive claims are redacted. Dialog identifiers that a binding such as PACE puts in its own track names are covered by that binding's privacy rules.¶

  • Trails and state are readable only by authorized parties (Section 12.2).¶

  • Authority rules run in the cell as directives, rather than on copies of the data.¶

  • Disclosures go only to authorities entitled to a class, encrypted to each, and every disclosure is itself a record in the trail.¶

  • Disclosure keys and archived disclosures SHOULD be tied to a purpose and a retention period, and destroyed when it ends.¶

  • Publishers SHOULD pad records to limit size analysis.¶

Redacted records are still visible in full to every party that can read the trail, so their unredacted claims, kinds, timing, and sizes, and the pattern of who talks to whom, remain visible to those readers and to relays and proxies. Deployments SHOULD redact every claim that is not needed by all readers.¶

  • [AAUTH] signs every agent request with HTTP Message Signatures and keeps a mission log.¶

  • [AUDIT-ARCH] proposes an architecture for auditing agent delegation and interaction traceability across domains.¶

TRAIL differs in four ways: the evidence is produced outside the agent at an attested edge; the edge acts only while its oversight is fresh; trails reach authorized parties the operator did not enumerate; and control (holds, gates, admission) returns on the same evidence plane. TRAIL aims to converge with these drafts on the record envelope, which all of them base on COSE or SCITT.¶

Appendix B. Examples

B.1. A Gated Payment Across Operators

  1. Operator O grants financial regulator F the directive types require-gate, require-level, and hold for action class "payment", and data class "finance". The grant is in O's state.¶

  2. F's state contains require-gate for class "payment" above a threshold, naming F as approver, and require-level of isolated for the same class.¶

  3. Agent A in cell C_A, in dialog D, asks to send a payment instruction to agent B.¶

  4. Edge A appends a gate-request record, with the amount redacted, and sends it with the "finance" disclosure encrypted to F as a directed record.¶

  5. F appends an approval record and sends it to C_A as a directed record.¶

  6. Edge A appends a send record that cites the approval and sends the call with the record attached.¶

  7. Edge B checks the record, A's level, and A's cosigned checkpoint, delivers the call, and appends a recv record citing A's.¶

  8. A health authority that can read the same trails sees that a payment happened, but cannot open the amount.¶

B.2. A Monitored Service

Regulator R publishes require-witness with itself as witness, a maximum age of 60 seconds, and service Z as counterparty. Z's edge rejects any call whose sender's latest checkpoint lacks an R cosignature younger than 60 seconds. R cosigns only trails it is receiving in full. If R stops watching, calls to Z stop within a minute.¶

B.3. A Swarm Size Cap

Admission issuer Q never has more than 1,000 valid admission tokens for swarm S. Q's require-admission directive makes peers reject calls from agents without a valid token hash. An agent spawned without a token has nobody to talk to among TRAIL peers. Retiring an agent releases its token, Q marks it invalid, and any witness can count valid tokens in Q's trail.¶

Appendix D. Open Issues

  • Which party runs the authorization server for reading a swarm's trails and state (Section 12.2).¶

  • Whether a receiver, or its authority, can require that a sender's operator has granted a named authority certain rights before calls are accepted. Today only witnesses can be required.¶

  • Whether verifiers publish Attestation Results themselves, in addition to edges carrying them in their state.¶

  • Extensions needed by further use cases, tracked in the issue tracker: holds that match everything descending from an input or from a PACE dialog through its parent_dialog_ids, gate responses that substitute an action, approvals signed with a passkey, records that cite a model server's attestation, and admission tokens that meter spend.¶

  • Default freshness budgets and grace periods per action class.¶

  • Whether one binding should be mandatory to implement for the evidence plane.¶

  • Whether the checkpoint and the tlog-checkpoint signed note can be one object, and how TRAIL witnesses relate to [TLOG-WITNESS].¶

  • A C4M claim that requires a given Property on every Object published under a scope, so that edge relays can enforce the presence of TRAIL_RECORD.¶

  • A Token Status List delivery profile over MOQT, for use beyond TRAIL.¶

  • Whether TRAIL_RECORD should be standardized as a general "per Object proof" Property for MOQT, with TRAIL as one user, which would amount to the first non-HTTP binding of the WIMSE Workload Proof Token.¶

  • Coordination with WIMSE and SCITT individual drafts on per-action evidence, to avoid incompatible record formats.¶

  • Measurements, for swarms of 1,000 and 10,000 agents, of the time from a hold being published to its enforcement over MOQT and over HTTP, the time from an authority going silent to covered agents pausing, trail throughput per edge, witness cosignature latency, and relay fan-out cost.¶
